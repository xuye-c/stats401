    5. Semantic Findings

    1. What are the major semantic topics?

    Nine K-means topics on the MiniLM vectors separate the bulletin into policy language versus curricular language. The largest groups are Social Science and Global Majors (71) and STEM Majors and Quantitative Courses (70), which sit in Part 10. Curriculum and Language Requirements (66) mixes general-education rules with EAP and writing. Admissions, Tuition, and Costs (63) and Dual Degrees, Transfer, and Graduation Credit (55) hold most of Parts 4–6. Mission, Community, and Campus Life (46) covers Part 1 and student affairs. Smaller topics are Chinese Language, Culture, and Campus Life (37), Course Load and Academic Standing (28), and a tight Major Electives cluster (17) of repeated “list will be updated periodically” blocks.

    2. Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

    Dual Degrees, Transfer, and Graduation Credit cuts across Academic Procedures, the Curriculum, and Career / Study Away. Admissions, Tuition, and Costs appears in Parts 4, 5, and 6. Curriculum and Language Requirements shows up in both Part 3 and major descriptions in Part 10. The matrix makes those shared columns dark in several rows, while Major Electives is almost only Part 10.

    3. Which formal sections contain the most semantically diverse content?

    Part 10 (Majors and Courses) and Part 6 (Academic Procedures) each occupy seven of the nine topics. Part 10 is diverse because STEM majors, social-science majors, language tracks, and elective boilerplate are semantically different even though they share one chapter. Part 6 is diverse because it mixes load/probation, transfer credit, withdrawal, and integrity-adjacent process language. Part 1 is the least diverse: all nine of its passages fall in Mission, Community, and Campus Life.

    4. Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

    Clicking a transfer-credit paragraph in Part 6 highlights neighbors in Part 8 (Course Approval and Credit Transfer) at cosine similarity about 0.89–0.94: both cap outside credit at 40 hours and discuss Duke S/U grades. Course-credit definitions in Part 3 also neighbor term-credit rules in Part 6 (about 0.92). The neighbor list under the map is the way to walk these pairs; many of the strongest matches are the same policy restated under a study-away heading.

    5. Which passages appear unusual relative to their formal section?

    In Part 10, three passages land in Mission, Community, and Campus Life instead of a major/STEM topic—for example the Media and Arts major’s opening on communication and creative expression, which reads more like institutional mission language than a course table. In Part 6, one STEM cluster point is a grading scale table remnant sitting among load and probation rules. Those outliers are easier to see when a matrix row is selected and a few map points sit far from that chapter’s main cloud.

    6. Does “credit” occur in one semantic region or across several topics?

    Searching credit lights up 275 passages across almost every topic, especially the two major clouds and Curriculum / Dual-degree columns. The word is not a single neighborhood: it means AP/IPC placement, 136 degree credits, CR/NC grading, transfer limits, and “4 credits” on a course line. graduation is narrower (29 passages), mostly Dual Degrees / Transfer and Course Load. registration is small and sits with admissions and costs. academic integrity is rare in this cleaned paragraph set and lands near mission/community and administrative process language rather than in a dedicated integrity cluster.

    6. Design Description and Findings

    Passages were embedded with sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (mean pooling, L2-normalized 384-d vectors). UMAP (n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.15, cosine, seed 401) is only a map of neighborhoods, not two interpretable axes. K-means (k=9, seed 401) ran on the original vectors. Cluster names come from TF-IDF terms plus reading representative paragraphs, not from the bulletin’s part titles, so a topic can cut across chapters.

    Color encodes topic; size encodes word count so long policy blocks are easier to click. Chapter is left to the matrix and the stroke of a selected point, which keeps the scatter from carrying two categorical colors. Search and filters dim rather than remove points so the global shape stays visible. Selecting a point lists five cosine neighbors and outlines the matching matrix cell; selecting a cell does the reverse. That coupling is the main analytic move: formal structure (rows) against semantic structure (columns and UMAP clouds). The map shows that majors split by division while credit policy is a bridge between procedures, curriculum, and study away. The matrix shows the same fact as shared dark cells. Together they support the finding that DKU’s bulletin is two documents in one: a relatively tight administrative handbook and a much larger, internally clustered catalog of majors.
