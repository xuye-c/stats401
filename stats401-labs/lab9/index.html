2025 GDP: Choropleth vs. Cartogram

Comparing geographic and quantitative representations of the world's 50 largest economies by nominal GDP.

1. Dataset Description

This visualization uses the provided 2025 nominal GDP dataset, based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025. GDP is measured in current U.S. dollars and represented in billions of U.S. dollars.

PART A

2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

PART C

4. Design and Comparison

The United States and China stand out most in both views; Germany, Japan, and India also receive strong choropleth colors. The log color scale makes differences among the other top economies legible despite the very large gap between the United States and smaller economies. In the cartogram, area is proportional to GDP, so the United States and China dominate much more strongly. Kazakhstan, Finland, and Peru, near the bottom of this top-50 dataset, shrink to small regions.

The choropleth preserves each country's geographic location, boundary, and relative land area, so it supports questions about regional patterns and where economies are situated. The cartogram scales projected country area directly by GDP and moves shapes slightly to reduce overlap. It therefore distorts boundaries, country size, and some spatial relationships, but supports comparison of economic magnitude more directly.

Changing from color to area makes the GDP hierarchy harder to ignore: large economies occupy more visual space rather than simply receiving a darker mark. That makes the cartogram effective for comparing economic weight, while the choropleth remains more reliable for geographic orientation and regional comparison. Neither view answers every question; the useful choice depends on whether the task prioritizes economic magnitude or geographic context.
