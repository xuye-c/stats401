2025 GDP: Choropleth vs. Cartogram

Comparing geographic and quantitative representations of the world's 50 largest economies by nominal GDP.

1. Dataset Description

This visualization uses the provided 2025 nominal GDP dataset, based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025. GDP is measured in current U.S. dollars and represented in billions of U.S. dollars. The CSV is joined to the world GeoJSON by ISO-3; explicit corrections for France and Norway's source placeholders produce 50 matching economies. WEO values may include IMF staff estimates and may be revised; countries outside the top 50 are treated as missing data, not zero GDP.

PARTS D–E

4. Design and Comparison

The United States and China stand out most in both views; Germany, Japan, and India also receive strong choropleth colors. The log color scale makes differences among the other top economies legible despite the very large gap between the United States and smaller economies. In the cartogram, area is proportional to GDP, so the United States and China dominate much more strongly. Kazakhstan, Finland, and Peru, near the bottom of this top-50 dataset, appear as small circles.

The choropleth preserves each country's geographic location and boundary, making it useful for regional patterns, neighboring-country comparisons, and geographic orientation. The cartogram replaces boundaries with circles whose areas encode GDP, then nudges them to reduce overlap. This distorts exact shapes and distances, but supports direct comparisons of economic magnitude.

Changing from color to area makes the GDP hierarchy harder to ignore: large economies occupy more visual space rather than simply receiving a darker mark. That makes the cartogram effective for comparing economic weight, while the choropleth remains more reliable for geographic orientation and regional comparison. Neither view answers every question; the useful choice depends on whether the task prioritizes economic magnitude or geographic context.
