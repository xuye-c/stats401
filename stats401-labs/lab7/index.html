Lab 7: Animated Temporal Commercial Network

1. Dataset Description

This assignment visualizes a synthetic 60-day commercial network among 12 companies. Each node is a company with a sector and a region. Each day’s records are undirected transactions: goods, shipping, components, materials, or services, with a dollar amount and a transaction count. The network is not a real market; it is built so that connectivity, activity, and cross-regional trade can be watched as time moves.

2. Temporal node-link visualization

Companies stay in regional columns (Asia, Europe, North America) so positions do not jump when the day’s links change. Play walks through all 60 days. Pause, reset, or drag the slider to inspect one day. Click a bar in the activity strip to jump to that day. New links fade in; inactive links fade out.

3. Encodings

  • Company sector — node color
  • Company region — node shape and column
  • That day’s transaction volume — node size
  • Transaction type — link color
  • Transaction amount — link width

Inactive companies stay on the map at a small size so the mental map is not rebuilt every day. A company that traded today has a brighter outline. Tooltips give the full company or transaction record for the current day.

4. Temporal network questions

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

Connectivity is low and bursty at first, then denser in the last third of the window. A typical day has about six links and nine active companies. The quietest days (1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19) have only three links and six companies, with totals near $40k–$49k. From about day 41 onward the graph thickens: several days reach 9–10 links and all 12 companies, and day 51 is the peak at about $203k. The activity strip under the network follows that rise. Adjacent days almost never share the same pairs, so the change is a daily swap of relationships rather than a slow thickening of a fixed edge set.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

Fusion Electronics (Technology, Asia) is the most persistent hub: it appears on 53 days and has the highest total volume. Early on, BlueRiver Logistics and Harbor Shipping are also busy. The late window is different. Ion Systems (Technology, North America) is modest through day 40, then its volume jumps in days 41–60 as it ties to Delta Components, Juniper Stores, Apex Textiles, and Fusion. Cedar Retail grows with it, adding late goods and materials links to Granite Wholesale and Keystone Materials. Evergreen Foods stays the smallest node: it only trades goods with Cedar Retail, then also with Lumen Distribution after day 42.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

Yes, especially early odd-numbered days. Days 1, 7, 13, and 19 are three disjoint pairs sitting inside regions: Cedar–Evergreen in North America, Granite–Harbor in Europe, and Ion–Lumen in North America. Mid-window days often split into a larger component of four to six companies plus one or two isolated pairs, with several firms idle. Peak late days (45, 51, 57, 60) still are not a single blob: they usually show one large component and one or two leftover pairs, but every company is active. The regional columns make those separate clusters easy to see without the layout reshuffling.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

Ten pairs repeat on 20 days and act like a backbone, even though they do not fire on consecutive days: Cedar–Evergreen goods, Ion–Lumen services, BlueRiver–Cedar shipping, Apex–Fusion components, Juniper–Lumen goods, and so on. Some mid-window services appear only in days 21–40 (Fusion with Harbor, Lumen, and Juniper). After day 41 new cross-regional ties show up and stay in the late rhythm: Cedar–Granite goods, Cedar–Keystone materials, Delta–Ion services, Apex–Ion, Fusion–Ion, and BlueRiver–Harbor shipping. Early pairs such as BlueRiver–Delta components and Cedar–Fusion services stop after day 20. Link width on a given day is that day’s amount, so a repeating pair can look stronger on some frames than others.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

More cross-regional. In the first 10 days about 54% of links already leave their region column, but several quiet days are entirely within-region (days 1, 7, 13, 19). The last 20 days average about 71–73% cross-regional links, and the new late pairs are mostly Asia–Europe, Asia–North America, or Europe–North America. Because companies are held in regional columns, a late frame shows more lines crossing the gaps than an early frame. Same-region trade does not disappear; it is just a smaller share of a busier graph.
